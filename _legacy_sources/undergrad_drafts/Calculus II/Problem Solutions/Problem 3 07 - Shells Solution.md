## Part 1: Finding the Volume Using the Shell Method

To find the volume of the solid generated by revolving this region around the $y$-axis, we use the cylindrical shell formula:

$$V = \int_{a}^{b} 2\pi \cdot (\text{radius}) \cdot (\text{height}) \, dx$$

### Step 1: Identify the Components

- **Radius:** The horizontal distance from the axis of rotation ($y$-axis) to any vertical slice at position $x$. This is $\text{radius} = x$.
    
- **Height:** Height must always be a positive length, measured as $\text{top boundary} - \text{bottom boundary}$.
    
    - The top boundary is the $x$-axis ($y = 0$).
        
    - The bottom boundary is the curve $y = -\sin(x^2)$.
        
    - Therefore, $\text{height} = 0 - (-\sin(x^2)) = \sin(x^2)$.
        
- **Limits of Integration ($a$ to $b$):** Looking at the graph, the region starts at the origin ($x = 0$) and ends at the dashed vertical line $x = \sqrt{\pi/2}$.
    

### Step 2: Set Up the Integral

Substitute the radius, height, and limits into the volume formula:

$$V = \int_{0}^{\sqrt{\pi/2}} 2\pi \cdot x \cdot \sin(x^2) \, dx$$

Pull the constant $2\pi$ outside the integral to clean it up:

$$V = 2\pi \int_{0}^{\sqrt{\pi/2}} x \sin(x^2) \, dx$$

## Part 2: Evaluating the Integral

### Step 3: Use $u$-Substitution

Because we have an $x^2$ inside the sine function and its derivative helper $x$ outside, $u$-substitution is the perfect tool here.

1. Let $u = x^2$
    
2. Find the derivative: $du = 2x \, dx \implies x \, dx = \frac{1}{2} \, du$
    
3. Change the integration limits to match $u$:
    
    - Lower limit: When $x = 0$, $u = 0^2 = 0$
        
    - Upper limit: When $x = \sqrt{\pi/2}$, $u = \left(\sqrt{\pi/2}\right)^2 = \frac{\pi}{2}$
        

Now rewrite the entire integral in terms of $u$:

$$V = 2\pi \int_{0}^{\pi/2} \sin(u) \cdot \left(\frac{1}{2} \, du\right)$$

The $\frac{1}{2}$ cancels out perfectly with the $2$ in $2\pi$:

$$V = \pi \int_{0}^{\pi/2} \sin(u) \, du$$

### Step 4: Integrate and Compute

The antiderivative of $\sin(u)$ is $-\cos(u)$:

$$V = \pi \Big[ -\cos(u) \Big]_{0}^{\pi/2}$$

Now apply the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus ($\text{upper limit} - \text{lower limit}$):

$$V = \pi \left( -\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right) - (-\cos(0)) \right)$$

Evaluate the trigonometric values:

- $\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right) = 0$
    
- $\cos(0) = 1$
    

Substitute them back into the equation:

$$V = \pi \left( 0 - (-1) \right) = \pi(1)$$

### Final Exact Answer:

$$V = \pi \text{ cubic units} \quad (\approx 3.1416)$$
