# Skill 3: Center of Mass, Moments, and Rotational Inertia in Three Dimensions (3D)

## 1. Topic Overview & Core Notes

### What is 3D Center of Mass and Rotational Inertia?

Extending balance and rotational calculations into three-dimensional space ($3\text{D}$) allows engineers to analyze real-world physical solids—such as rocket nose cones, jet turbine shafts, satellite components, and concrete support pillars—before building physical prototypes.

- **3D Center of Mass $(\overline{x}, \overline{y}, \overline{z})$:** The single spatial point where the total mass $M$ of a 3D solid $E$ acts.
    
- **3D Moments of Inertia ($I_x, I_y, I_z$):** Measure a solid's resistance to angular acceleration around the $x$-, $y$-, or $z$-axes. In 3D, the distance to an axis depends on two coordinates squared (e.g., distance squared to the $z$-axis is $x^2 + y^2$).
    

### Essential Formulas

#### 1. Total Mass ($M$)

Accumulates material density $\rho(x,y,z)$ across the 3D volume $E$:

$$M = \iiint_E \rho(x,y,z) \, dV$$

#### 2. First Moments about Coordinate Planes

First moments measure spatial leverage relative to the coordinate planes ($xy$, $xz$, and $yz$):

- **Moment about $yz$-plane ($M_{yz}$):** Measures horizontal leverage along $x$:
    
    $$M_{yz} = \iiint_E x \, \rho(x,y,z) \, dV$$
    
- **Moment about $xz$-plane ($M_{xz}$):** Measures horizontal leverage along $y$:
    
    $$M_{xz} = \iiint_E y \, \rho(x,y,z) \, dV$$
    
- **Moment about $xy$-plane ($M_{xy}$):** Measures vertical leverage along $z$:
    
    $$M_{xy} = \iiint_E z \, \rho(x,y,z) \, dV$$
    

#### 3. Center of Mass Coordinates $(\overline{x}, \overline{y}, \overline{z})$

$$\overline{x} = \frac{M_{yz}}{M}, \quad \overline{y} = \frac{M_{xz}}{M}, \quad \overline{z} = \frac{M_{xy}}{M}$$

#### 4. Second Moments of Inertia (3D)

Measures rotational resistance about the major spatial axes:

- **About the x-axis ($I_x$):** Distance squared is $y^2 + z^2$:
    
    $$I_x = \iiint_E (y^2 + z^2) \, \rho(x,y,z) \, dV$$
    
- **About the y-axis ($I_y$):** Distance squared is $x^2 + z^2$:
    
    $$I_y = \iiint_E (x^2 + z^2) \, \rho(x,y,z) \, dV$$
    
- **About the z-axis ($I_z$):** Distance squared is $x^2 + y^2$:
    
    $$I_z = \iiint_E (x^2 + y^2) \, \rho(x,y,z) \, dV$$
    

### Choosing the Right 3D Coordinate System

|**System**|**Differential Volume (dV)**|**Best Geometry**|**Key Transformations**|
|---|---|---|---|
|**Rectangular**|$dV = dz \, dy \, dx$|Cubes, boxes, sloped planes|Direct $x, y, z$|
|**Cylindrical**|$dV = r \, dz \, dr \, d\theta$|Cylinders, cones, paraboloids, drive shafts|$x = r\cos\theta, \, y = r\sin\theta, \, x^2+y^2 = r^2$|
|**Spherical**|$dV = \rho^2 \sin\phi \, d\rho \, d\phi \, d\theta$|Spheres, hemispheres, domes|$x = \rho\sin\phi\cos\theta, \, y = \rho\sin\phi\sin\theta, \, z = \rho\cos\phi$|

> **Critical Jacobian Reminder:** Always include the Jacobian factor **$r$** for cylindrical coordinates and **$\rho^2 \sin\phi$** for spherical coordinates!

### Step-by-Step Solution Roadmap

1. **Visualize & Define Solid $E$:** Sketch the 3D shape and identify bounding surfaces.
    
2. **Select Coordinate System:** Match rectangular, cylindrical, or spherical coordinates to the boundary equations.
    
3. **Exploit Symmetry:** If a solid and its density are symmetric about a plane or axis, the center of mass lies on that symmetry plane/axis.
    
4. **Evaluate Triple Integrals:** Solve $M$, first moments ($M_{yz}, M_{xz}, M_{xy}$), and rotational inertia ($I_x, I_y, I_z$).
    
5. **Divide for Center Coordinates:** Find $(\overline{x}, \overline{y}, \overline{z}) = (M_{yz}/M, M_{xz}/M, M_{xy}/M)$.
    

### Common Pitfalls to Avoid

- **Notation Collision ($\rho$ vs. $\rho$):** Do not confuse the material density function $\rho(x,y,z)$ with the spherical radius coordinate $\rho$.
    
- **Forgetting 3D Distance Terms:** In 2D, $I_x$ uses $y^2$. In 3D, distance from the x-axis depends on both $y$ and $z$, so $I_x$ uses $y^2 + z^2$.
    
- **Mixing First & Second Moments:** First moments ($M_{xy}$) find the center of mass; second moments ($I_z$) measure rotational inertia.
    

## 2. Standard Mathematical Practice Problems

### Problem 1: Rectangular Solid with Vertical Density Gradient

A solid block occupies $0 \le x \le 1$, $0 \le y \le 2$, and $0 \le z \le 3$. Its density varies vertically:

$$\rho(x,y,z) = z$$

Find the total mass $M$, the center of mass coordinates $(\overline{x}, \overline{y}, \overline{z})$, and the moment of inertia $I_z$ about the z-axis.

#### Solution:

**Step 1: Compute Total Mass ($M$)**

$$M = \int_{0}^{1} \int_{0}^{2} \int_{0}^{3} z \, dz \, dy \, dx = \left(\int_{0}^{1} dx\right) \left(\int_{0}^{2} dy\right) \left(\int_{0}^{3} z \, dz\right)$$

$$M = (1) \cdot (2) \cdot \left[ \frac{z^2}{2} \right]_0^3 = 2 \cdot \frac{9}{2} = 9$$

**Step 2: Compute Center of Mass Coordinates $(\overline{x}, \overline{y}, \overline{z})$**

- **By horizontal symmetry:** Because density depends only on $z$, mass is evenly split across $x \in [0,1]$ and $y \in [0,2]$:
    
    $$\overline{x} = 0.5, \quad \overline{y} = 1.0$$
    
- **Compute $M_{xy}$ for $\overline{z}$:**
    
    $$M_{xy} = \int_{0}^{1} \int_{0}^{2} \int_{0}^{3} z(z) \, dz \, dy \, dx = (1)(2) \left[ \frac{z^3}{3} \right]_0^3 = 2(9) = 18$$
    
    $$\overline{z} = \frac{M_{xy}}{M} = \frac{18}{9} = 2.0$$
    

**Step 3: Compute Moment of Inertia $I_z$**

$$I_z = \int_{0}^{1} \int_{0}^{2} \int_{0}^{3} (x^2 + y^2)z \, dz \, dy \, dx = \left(\int_{0}^{3} z \, dz\right) \int_{0}^{1} \int_{0}^{2} (x^2 + y^2) \, dy \, dx$$

$$\int_{0}^{3} z \, dz = \frac{9}{2}$$

$$\int_{0}^{1} \int_{0}^{2} (x^2 + y^2) \, dy \, dx = \int_{0}^{1} \left[ x^2 y + \frac{y^3}{3} \right]_0^2 \, dx = \int_{0}^{1} \left(2x^2 + \frac{8}{3}\right) \, dx = \left[ \frac{2x^3}{3} + \frac{8x}{3} \right]_0^1 = \frac{10}{3}$$

$$I_z = \frac{9}{2} \cdot \frac{10}{3} = 15$$

$$\mathbf{M = 9, \quad (\overline{x}, \overline{y}, \overline{z}) = (0.5, 1.0, 2.0), \quad I_z = 15}$$

### Problem 2: Uniform Cylinder in Cylindrical Coordinates

A solid cylinder of radius $R = 2$ and height $h = 4$ rests on $z = 0$ centered along the z-axis ($0 \le r \le 2, \, 0 \le z \le 4$). Assuming a uniform density $\rho = 3$, compute $M$, $I_z$, and $k_z$.

#### Solution:

**Step 1: Compute Total Mass ($M$)**

$$M = \int_{0}^{2\pi} \int_{0}^{2} \int_{0}^{4} 3 \cdot r \, dz \, dr \, d\theta = 3 \left(\int_{0}^{2\pi} d\theta\right) \left(\int_{0}^{4} dz\right) \left(\int_{0}^{2} r \, dr\right)$$

$$M = 3(2\pi)(4)\left[\frac{r^2}{2}\right]_0^2 = 24\pi(2) = 48\pi \approx 150.80$$

**Step 2: Compute $I_z$ (using $x^2 + y^2 = r^2$)**

$$I_z = \int_{0}^{2\pi} \int_{0}^{2} \int_{0}^{4} (r^2) \cdot 3 \cdot r \, dz \, dr \, d\theta = 3(2\pi)(4) \int_{0}^{2} r^3 \, dr = 24\pi \left[\frac{r^4}{4}\right]_0^2 = 24\pi(4) = 96\pi \approx 301.59$$

**Step 3: Compute Radius of Gyration ($k_z$)**

$$k_z = \sqrt{\frac{I_z}{M}} = \sqrt{\frac{96\pi}{48\pi}} = \sqrt{2} \approx 1.414$$

$$\mathbf{M = 48\pi \approx 150.80, \quad I_z = 96\pi \approx 301.59, \quad k_z = \sqrt{2} \approx 1.414}$$
