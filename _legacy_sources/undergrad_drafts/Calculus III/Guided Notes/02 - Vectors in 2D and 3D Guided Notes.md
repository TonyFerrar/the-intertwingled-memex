## Engineering Application 
In real-life engineering, these three skills form the mathematical framework required to analyze and interact with the physical 3D world. **Plotting points in 3D (Skill 1)** provides the foundation for automated manufacturing, enabling CNC machines, 3D printers, and robotic arms to navigate to precise spatial coordinates. Engineers build on this by using **vector notation (Skill 2)** to define physical forces that have both magnitude and direction, such as calculating the exact tension components ($\hat{i}, \hat{j}, \hat{k}$) in bridge support cables or tracking the aerodynamic forces acting on an aircraft. Finally, **vector arithmetic (Skill 3)** allows systems to compute the combined impact of multiple interacting forces, which is essential for maritime autopilot systems calculating a ship's true trajectory through a crosscurrent, civil engineers balancing structural wind loads, and game engines calculating real-time 3D physics.
