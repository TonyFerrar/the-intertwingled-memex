
## Engineering Applications
In engineering, double integrals are vital for modeling variables distributed across two-dimensional surfaces. Optoelectronic engineers use them over rectangular and general boundaries to analyze local light intensity and calculate the average irradiance across LED panels or solar arrays, while civil and structural engineers evaluate double integrals over general regions to determine the exact centroids, mass distributions, and hydrostatic forces acting against non-uniform plates or curved dams. Furthermore, aerospace and mechanical engineers frequently switch the order of integration to simplify complex algebraic boundary functions when calculating moments of inertia and rotational resistance for intricate components like airplane wings or ship hulls.
