Here is the step-by-step solution to evaluate the definite integral using geometric formulas.

$$\int_{1}^{7} (3 - |-4 + x|) \, dx$$

### Step 1: Identify the Geometric Shape

The definite integral represents the net signed area bounded between the function $f(x) = 3 - |-4 + x|$ and the $x$-axis from $x = 1$ to $x = 7$.

Looking at the graph:

- Between $x = 1$ and $x = 7$, the function sits entirely **above** the $x$-axis ($y \ge 0$).
    
- The shape formed under the curve over this specific interval is a **triangle**.
    

### Step 2: Determine the Dimensions of the Triangle

To find the area of this triangle, we need its **base** and its **height**:

1. **Base ($b$):** The triangle starts on the $x$-axis at $x = 1$ and ends at $x = 7$.
    
    $$\text{Base} = 7 - 1 = 6$$
    
2. **Height ($h$):** The peak (vertex) of the triangle is located at $x = 4$. Evaluating the function at this point gives the maximum vertical height:
    
    $$f(4) = 3 - |-4 + 4| = 3 - 0 = 3$$
    
    $$\text{Height} = 3$$
    

### Step 3: Apply the Geometric Area Formula

Using the standard formula for the area of a triangle:

$$\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2} \times \text{base} \times \text{height}$$

Substitute the values we found:

$$\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2} \times 6 \times 3$$

$$\text{Area} = 3 \times 3 = 9$$

### Final Answer

$$9$$
