**Context:** A construction tower crane lifting a heavy concrete beam exerts a downward vertical force vector due to gravity of $\vec{F}_g = \langle 0, 0, -50 \rangle$ kilonewtons (kN) on its central structural support node. Simultaneously, a severe storm hits the construction site, generating a horizontal wind load vector hitting the structure at $\vec{F}_w = \langle 12, -5, 0 \rangle$ kN.

**Task:** 1. Find the total combined force vector $\vec{F}_{\text{total}}$ acting on the crane's node.

2. If structural safety parameters dictate that this node can withstand a maximum total force magnitude of $55\text{ kN}$ before collapsing, determine if the structure is safe.

**Mathematical Solution:**

1. **Combined Force Vector:** Sum the vectors component-by-component:
    
    $$\vec{F}_{\text{total}} = \vec{F}_g + \vec{F}_w = \langle 0+12, 0+(-5), -50+0 \rangle = \langle 12, -5, -50 \rangle\text{ kN}$$
    
2. **Calculate Total Magnitude:**
    
    $$|\vec{F}_{\text{total}}| = \sqrt{12^2 + (-5)^2 + (-50)^2}$$
    
    $$|\vec{F}_{\text{total}}| = \sqrt{144 + 25 + 2500} = \sqrt{2669} \approx 51.66\text{ kN}$$
    

**Real-Life Application Meaning:**

The calculated total load magnitude acting on the crane's structural node is $51.66\text{ kN}$. Because this value is less than the critical engineering failure threshold of $55\text{ kN}$, the crane structure will technically withstand the storm forces without an immediate structural collapse. Civil and structural engineers run these multi-force vector additions daily to ensure that buildings, cranes, and scaffolding remain safe under extreme weather conditions. Knowing the total vector magnitude allows them to declare site safety zones or decide when a storm has become severe enough to evacuate workers.
