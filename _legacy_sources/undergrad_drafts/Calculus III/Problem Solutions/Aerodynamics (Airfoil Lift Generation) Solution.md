An aerospace engineer is modeling the velocity vector field of air moving around a cross-section of an experimental aircraft wing inside a wind tunnel. The velocity field within the control region is given by:

$$\mathbf{v}(x,y) = \langle 2y - \sin x, 5x + e^y \rangle$$

To calculate the upward lift force generated by the airfoil section, the engineer must find the fluid circulation around the boundary $C$ of the rectangular test zone defined by $0 \le x \le \pi$ and $0 \le y \le 2$. Use Green's Theorem to compute the net circulation.

#### Step-by-Step Solution:

- **Step 1: Map components and limits.**
    
    $$P(x,y) = 2y - \sin x, \quad Q(x,y) = 5x + e^y$$
    
    The integration limits for the rectangular region are $0 \le x \le \pi$ and $0 \le y \le 2$.
    
- **Step 2: Compute cross-partials.**
    
    $$\frac{\partial P}{\partial y} = \frac{\partial}{\partial y}(2y - \sin x) = 2$$
    
    $$\frac{\partial Q}{\partial x} = \frac{\partial}{\partial x}(5x + e^y) = 5$$
    
- **Step 3: Apply Green's Theorem formula.**
    
    $$\frac{\partial Q}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial P}{\partial y} = 5 - 2 = 3$$
    
    $$\oint_{C} \mathbf{v} \cdot d\mathbf{r} = \int_{0}^{2} \int_{0}^{\pi} 3 \, dx \, dy$$
    
- **Step 4: Perform the integration.**
    
    $$\text{Inner Integral: } \int_{0}^{\pi} 3 \, dx = 3\pi$$
    
    $$\text{Outer Integral: } \int_{0}^{2} 3\pi \, dy = [3\pi y]_0^2 = 6\pi$$
    

#### Meaning of the Answer and Engineering Application:

The mathematical answer of $6\pi$ represents a net counterclockwise fluid circulation value around the wing section. In fluid mechanics, the Kutta-Joukowski theorem states that the physical lift force experienced by an airfoil is directly proportional to this circulation value.

Because the calculated circulation is positive, it mathematically confirms that the air is moving faster over the top surface of the wing than the bottom surface, producing a low-pressure zone above the wing. The aerospace engineer uses this exact numerical result to verify that the wing profile successfully generates the required vertical lift force to safely overcome the aircraft's weight during takeoff.
