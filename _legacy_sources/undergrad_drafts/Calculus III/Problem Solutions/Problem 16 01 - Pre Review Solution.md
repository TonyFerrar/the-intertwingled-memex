Here is the step-by-step solution to evaluate the definite integral using geometric formulas.

$$\int_{-7}^{-3} \left(\sqrt{-(x + 5)^2 + 4}\right) dx$$

### Step 1: Identify the Geometric Shape

The definite integral represents the net signed area under the curve of $f(x) = \sqrt{-(x + 5)^2 + 4}$ from $x = -7$ to $x = -3$.

If we set $y = \sqrt{-(x + 5)^2 + 4}$ and square both sides, we can rewrite the equation:

$$y^2 = -(x + 5)^2 + 4$$

$$(x + 5)^2 + y^2 = 4$$

This is the standard equation of a circle:

- **Center:** $(-5, 0)$
    
- **Radius ($r$):** $\sqrt{4} = 2$
    

Since the original function specifies the positive square root ($y \ge 0$), the graph shows the top half of this circle—a **semicircle**—sitting completely above the x-axis.

### Step 2: Check the Limits of Integration

The bounds of integration are from $x = -7$ to $x = -3$:

- The leftmost point of the semicircle is at $x = -5 - 2 = -7$.
    
- The rightmost point of the semicircle is at $x = -5 + 2 = -3$.
    

Because the interval $[-7, -3]$ spans the entire width of the shape, the value of the definite integral is exactly equal to the total area of this semicircle.

### Step 3: Apply the Geometric Area Formula

The area of a semicircle is half the area of a full circle:

$$\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2} \pi r^2$$

Substitute the radius $r = 2$ into the formula:

$$\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2} \pi (2)^2$$

$$\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2} \pi (4) = 2\pi$$

### Final Answer

$$2\pi$$
