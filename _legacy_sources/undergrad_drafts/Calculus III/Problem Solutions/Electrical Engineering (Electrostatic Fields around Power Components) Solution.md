An electrical engineer maps out the local electric field $\mathbf{E}$ (in Newtons per Coulomb) surrounding a specialized high-voltage power transmission component. The field vector map is recorded as:

$$\mathbf{E}(x,y,z) = (x^2)\mathbf{i} + (y^2)\mathbf{j} + (z^2)\mathbf{k}$$

Evaluate $\nabla \cdot \mathbf{E}$ and $\nabla \times \mathbf{E}$ at the coordinate point $(1, 1, 1)$, and translate what these findings reveal about the local electrical charges.

#### **Solution:**

1. **Compute Divergence:**
    
    $$\nabla \cdot \mathbf{E} = \frac{\partial}{\partial x}(x^2) + \frac{\partial}{\partial y}(y^2) + \frac{\partial}{\partial z}(z^2) = 2x + 2y + 2z$$
    
    Evaluating at $(1,1,1)$:
    
    $$\nabla \cdot \mathbf{E}(1,1,1) = 2(1) + 2(1) + 2(1) = 6$$
    
2. **Compute Curl:**
    
    - $\mathbf{i}\text{-component}: \frac{\partial}{\partial y}(z^2) - \frac{\partial}{\partial z}(y^2) = 0 - 0 = 0$
        
    - $\mathbf{j}\text{-component}: \frac{\partial}{\partial z}(x^2) - \frac{\partial}{\partial x}(z^2) = 0 - 0 = 0$
        
    - $\mathbf{k}\text{-component}: \frac{\partial}{\partial x}(y^2) - \frac{\partial}{\partial y}(x^2) = 0 - 0 = 0$
        
        $$\nabla \times \mathbf{E} = \mathbf{0}$$
        

#### **Meaning of the Answer & Real-Life Application:**

The calculations show a positive divergence of **$6$** and a curl of **$\mathbf{0}$**.

- **The Translation:** According to Gauss's Law, the divergence of an electric field is directly proportional to the local electrical charge density. Because the divergence is positive ($6$), this specific point in space acts as a net **electrical source**, meaning a physical concentration of positive electrical charge is stationed right at $(1, 1, 1)$, pushing field lines outward.
    
- **The Application:** Because the curl is exactly the zero vector ($\mathbf{0}$), the field is completely **irrotational** (conservative). This tells the engineer that the electric field lines radiate strictly outward in straight lines from the charge center without looping or curling. This confirms that the field is entirely electrostatic, generated by stationary physical charges rather than induced by a dangerous, fluctuating magnetic field. The engineer can safely use standard voltage drop equations to design the component's surrounding insulation shields without worrying about unexpected inductive current loops.
