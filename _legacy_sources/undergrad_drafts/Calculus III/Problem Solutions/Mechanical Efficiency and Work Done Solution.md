**Problem Statement:** A mechanical conveyor system uses an electric motor to pull a heavy cargo crate along a flat, horizontal tracking rail. The displacement vector of the crate is $\vec{d} = 12\hat{i}\text{ meters}$. Due to the structural positioning of the mechanical pulley, the cable pulls on the crate with a constant force vector of $\vec{F} = 350\hat{i} + 120\hat{j}\text{ Newtons}$. Compute the work done on the crate using the dot product.

**Solution:**

1. Set up the dot product equation for work:
    
    $$W = \vec{F} \cdot \vec{d}$$
    
2. Substitute the component values:
    
    $$W = (350\hat{i} + 120\hat{j}) \cdot (12\hat{i} + 0\hat{j})$$
    
    $$W = (350 \cdot 12) + (120 \cdot 0)$$
    
    $$W = 4200 + 0 = 4200\text{ Joules (J)}$$
    

**Meaning of the Answer and Engineering Application:**

The result of $4200\text{ J}$ represents the total scalar mechanical energy transferred to the crate to move it across the track. Notice that the $120\hat{j}\text{ N}$ vertical component of the force vector contributed exactly $0\text{ J}$ of work. In real-life machine design, this tells the engineer that any energy spent generating vertical force is completely wasted with respect to horizontal travel. Minimizing the vertical angle of the cable would align the force vector closer with the displacement path, increasing mechanical efficiency.
