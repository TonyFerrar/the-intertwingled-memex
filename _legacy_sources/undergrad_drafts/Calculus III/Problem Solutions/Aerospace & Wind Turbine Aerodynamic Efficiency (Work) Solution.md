**Problem Statement:** A wind turbine blade is rotating, and at a specific structural section, the local displacement direction of the blade segment is along the vector $\vec{d} = 2\hat{i} + 5\hat{j}\text{ meters}$. The aerodynamic lift force vector generated by the wind passing over this blade section is $\vec{F} = 120\hat{i} - 30\hat{j}\text{ Newtons}$. Compute the mechanical work done by the wind on this blade section.

**Solution:**

1. Apply the work equation via the dot product:
    
    $$W = \vec{F} \cdot \vec{d}$$
    
2. Substitute the given component values:
    
    $$W = (120)(2) + (-30)(5)$$
    
    $$W = 240 - 150$$
    
    $$W = 90\text{ J}$$
    

**Meaning of the Answer and Engineering Application:**

The result of $90\text{ J}$ means that the wind successfully transferred $90\text{ Joules}$ of scalar kinetic energy into the turbine blade segment along its path of motion. Notice that because the force components partially opposed each other (as shown by the negative $\hat{j}$ term), some potential energy was canceled out. Renewable energy engineers calculate this exact dot product to optimize the twist angle (pitch) of turbine blades. By ensuring the lift force vector aligns as closely as possible with the blade's travel vector $\vec{d}$, they maximize the work output, leading to higher electrical power generation.
