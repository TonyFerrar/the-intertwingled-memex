Here is the step-by-step solution to find the total area between the graph of the function and the $x$-axis.

### Step 1: Understand the Definition of "Total Area"

Unlike a definite integral (which computes _net_ signed area, where regions below the $x$-axis are negative), the **total area** treats all regions as positive.

Looking at the graph, the total area is divided into two distinct triangular regions:

1. **Triangle 1:** Located above the $x$-axis from $x = 0$ to $x = 4$.
    
2. **Triangle 2:** Located below the $x$-axis from $x = 4$ to $x = 8$.
    

We will calculate the area of each triangle using the geometric formula $\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2} \times \text{base} \times \text{height}$ and add them together.

### Step 2: Calculate the Area of Triangle 1 (from $x = 0$ to $x = 4$)

- **Base ($b_1$):** Spans along the $x$-axis from $0$ to $4 \implies b_1 = 4 - 0 = 4$.
    
- **Height ($h_1$):** Reaches up to a peak $y$-value of $1 \implies h_1 = 1$.
    

$$\text{Area}_1 = \frac{1}{2} \times 4 \times 1 = 2$$

### Step 3: Calculate the Area of Triangle 2 (from $x = 4$ to $x = 8$)

- **Base ($b_2$):** Spans along the $x$-axis from $4$ to $8 \implies b_2 = 8 - 4 = 4$.
    
- **Height ($h_2$):** Reaches down to a $y$-value of $-3$. Since area measurements are strictly positive, the height is its absolute vertical distance $\implies h_2 = 3$.
    

$$\text{Area}_2 = \frac{1}{2} \times 4 \times 3 = 6$$

### Step 4: Sum the Areas

Add the individual geometric areas together to get the total area:

$$A = \text{Area}_1 + \text{Area}_2$$

$$A = 2 + 6 = 8$$

$$8$$
