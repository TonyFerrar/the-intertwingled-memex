Here is the step-by-step solution to evaluate $\sin\left(\frac{3\pi}{4}\right)$ in exact form.

### Step 1: Identify the Quadrant

The given angle is $\theta = \frac{3\pi}{4}$ radians.

- Since $\frac{\pi}{2} < \frac{3\pi}{4} < \pi$, the angle lies in **Quadrant II**.
    
- In Quadrant II, the sine function value is **positive** ($\text{S}$ in the "All Students Take Calculus" mnemonic).
    

### Step 2: Find the Reference Angle

The reference angle ($\theta_R$) is the acute angle formed between the terminal side of the angle and the x-axis. For an angle in Quadrant II, we calculate it as:

$$\theta_R = \pi - \theta$$

Substituting our value:

$$\theta_R = \pi - \frac{3\pi}{4} = \frac{\pi}{4}$$

### Step 3: Evaluate Using the Special Angle

Now, evaluate the sine function at the reference angle $\frac{\pi}{4}$ ($45^\circ$).

From the standard unit circle or standard special right triangles ($45^\circ$-$45^\circ$-$90^\circ$):

$$\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{4}\right) = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$$

### Step 4: Apply the Sign of the Quadrant

Since the angle is in Quadrant II where sine is positive, the value retains its positive sign:

$$\sin\left(\frac{3\pi}{4}\right) = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$$

### Final Answer

$$\sin\left(\frac{3\pi}{4}\right) = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$$
