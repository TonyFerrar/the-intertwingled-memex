### 1. Find Intersection Points

To find where the curves intersect, set the two equations equal to each other:

$$x^2 = 2x$$

Rearrange the equation to solve for $x$:

$$x^2 - 2x = 0$$

$$x(x - 2) = 0$$

This gives the $x$-coordinates of the intersection points:

- $x = 0$
    
- $x = 2$
    

Substituting these back into either original equation gives the full intersection coordinates:

- **$(0, 0)$**
    
- **$(2, 4)$**
    

### 2. Decide Top/Bottom on the Interval

The interval of interest is between the intersection points, which is $[0, 2]$.

To determine which curve is on top, pick a test point inside the interval, such as $x = 1$:

- For $y = 2x$: $y = 2(1) = 2$
    
- For $y = x^2$: $y = (1)^2 = 1$
    

Since $2 > 1$, the line is above the parabola on this interval:

- **Top function:** $y = 2x$
    
- **Bottom function:** $y = x^2$
    

### 3. Write the Double Integral for the Area

The area $A$ of a region using a double integral in rectangular coordinates is given by $\iint_R dy \, dx$.

Using our boundaries, the inner integral limits go from the bottom function to the top function ($y = x^2$ to $y = 2x$), and the outer integral limits cover the $x$-range from $0$ to $2$:

$$A = \int_{0}^{2} \int_{x^2}^{2x} dy \, dx$$
