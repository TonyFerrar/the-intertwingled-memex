Given the vector field:
$$F(x,y) = \langle xy, x^2 - y \rangle$$

---

### Step 1: Find $F(2,1)$
To evaluate the vector field at the specific point $(x, y) = (2, 1)$, substitute $x = 2$ and $y = 1$ into the component functions:

* **$P(2,1)$ component:** $2 \cdot 1 = 2$
* **$Q(2,1)$ component:** $2^2 - 1 = 4 - 1 = 3$

$$F(2,1) = \langle 2, 3 \rangle$$

---

### Step 2: Find $\|F(2,1)\|$
The magnitude (or length) of the vector at this point is calculated using the Euclidean norm formula:

$$\|F(2,1)\| = \sqrt{P^2 + Q^2}$$
$$\|F(2,1)\| = \sqrt{2^2 + 3^2} = \sqrt{4 + 9} = \sqrt{13}$$

---

### Step 3: Interpretation
* **Vector Value:** At the specific point $(2, 1)$ in the 2D plane, the vector field assigns a vector that points $2$ units to the right and $3$ units up.
* **Magnitude:** The strength, speed, or intensity of the field at that specific coordinate is exactly $\sqrt{13} \approx 3.61$ units.
