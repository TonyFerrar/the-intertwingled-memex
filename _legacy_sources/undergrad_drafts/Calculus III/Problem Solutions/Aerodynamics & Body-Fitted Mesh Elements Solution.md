In Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD), aerodynamicists analyze air pressure variations along the curved surfaces of airplane wings using body-fitted coordinate meshes. Suppose an aerospace engineer isolates a single, non-rectangular mesh cell $R$ adjacent to a wing profile. The cell boundaries are defined by lines tracking the fluid streamlines and normal curves: $y - 2x = 0$, $y - 2x = 3$, $y + x = 1$, and $y + x = 4$. 

The local aerodynamic lifting force density per unit area acting within this cell is modeled by the function $f(x,y) = y - 2x$ (measured in Newtons per square meter, $\text{N/m}^2$). Calculate the total lift force generated strictly across this single mesh element.
**Given Transformation:** $u = y - 2x$ and $v = y + x$.

#### Solution:
1. **Determine the New Limits:**
   * $y - 2x = 0 \implies u = 0$
   * $y - 2x = 3 \implies u = 3$
   * $y + x = 1 \implies v = 1$
   * $y + x = 4 \implies v = 4$
   
   Limits are $0 \le u \le 3$ and $1 \le v \le 4$.

2. **Compute the Jacobian:**
   $$\frac{\partial(u,v)}{\partial(x,y)} = \det \begin{bmatrix} -2 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{bmatrix} = (-2)(1) - (1)(1) = -3$$
   $$|J| = \left|-\frac{1}{3}\right| = \frac{1}{3}$$

3. **Set Up and Evaluate the Integral:**
   The force density function transforms directly to $u$.
   $$\text{Total Force} = \int_{1}^{4} \int_{0}^{3} u \cdot \left(\frac{1}{3}\right) \, du \, dv$$
   $$\int_{0}^{3} \frac{1}{3}u \, du = \left[ \frac{1}{6}u^2 \right]_{0}^{3} = \frac{9}{6} = \frac{3}{2}$$
   $$\int_{1}^{4} \frac{3}{2} \, dv = \frac{3}{2}(4 - 1) = \frac{9}{2} = 4.5$$

#### Meaning & Real-Life Application:
* **Meaning of the Answer:** The calculated value of $4.5$ means that exactly $4.5\text{ Newtons}$ of lift force are generated by the air passing over this specific localized patch of the wing surface.
* **Engineering Application:** In practice, CFD software performs this exact transformation and Jacobian scaling across millions of individual skewed elements tracking a vehicle's body. By integrating each patch cleanly in a standardized $uv$ grid, the computer can rapidly sum up the localized forces to determine the overall lift, drag, and structural flight loads of the entire aircraft.
