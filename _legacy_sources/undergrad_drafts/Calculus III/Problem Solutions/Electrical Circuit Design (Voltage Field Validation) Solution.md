An electrical engineer is mapping an electrostatic field within a microchip substrate to ensure stable voltage delivery. The localized electric field vector field is modeled by the equation:

$$\mathbf{E}(x,y) = \langle y^2 + 2x, 2xy + 3y^2 \rangle \quad \text{defined on } \mathbb{R}^2$$

1. Determine whether $\mathbf{E}(x,y)$ represents a valid conservative electrostatic field.
    
2. Based on your result, evaluate the total electromotive accumulation $\oint_C \mathbf{E} \cdot d\mathbf{r}$ along a closed-loop copper trace circuit path $C$ etched on the microchip.
    

#### Step-by-Step Solution:

- **Step 1:** Identify components of the electric field:
    
    $$P(x,y) = y^2 + 2x, \quad Q(x,y) = 2xy + 3y^2$$
    
- **Step 2:** Calculate cross-partials:
    
    $$P_y = \frac{\partial}{\partial y}(y^2 + 2x) = 2y$$
    
    $$Q_x = \frac{\partial}{\partial x}(2xy + 3y^2) = 2y$$
    
- **Step 3:** Since $P_y = Q_x = 2y$ everywhere on the solid plane $\mathbb{R}^2$, the field is conservative.
    
- **Step 4:** Because the field is verified as conservative, any closed-loop line integral yields a net total of zero:
    
    $$\oint_C \mathbf{E} \cdot d\mathbf{r} = 0$$
    

#### Meaning of the Answer and Engineering Application:

The mathematical answer of zero directly validates **Kirchhoff's Voltage Law (KVL)**, which dictates that the net sum of all electrical potential differences around any closed circuit loop must equal zero. In practice, this confirms to the engineer that the microchip layout represents a stable, ideal electrostatic system where energy is perfectly conserved. Because the field is path-independent, no stray voltage or phantom energy sinks are spontaneously generated by the background field configuration as current moves through the circuit trace loops.
