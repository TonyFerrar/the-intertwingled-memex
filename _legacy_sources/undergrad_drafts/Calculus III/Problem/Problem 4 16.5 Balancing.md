Shifting to Polar Coordinates for Circular Parts (Level: Intermediate)

**Scenario:** A curved camera rig component or circular balance ring needs its center of mass calculated.

- **The Problem:** A semi-circular thin plate occupies the upper half of a disk, bounded by the circles $r = 1$ and $r = 3$ for $0 \le \theta \le \pi$. Assuming a uniform density $\rho = \rho_0$, use polar coordinates to locate its center of mass $(\overline{x}, \overline{y})$.
    
- **What this tests:** Recognizing when rectangular boundaries are messy and cleanly converting to polar coordinates ($dA = r \, dr \, d\theta$).
    
- **Symmetry Shortcut:** Look closely at the geometry across the y-axis. Can you determine $\overline{x}$ without doing any integration at all?
