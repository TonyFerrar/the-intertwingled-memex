Planetary / Spherical Distribution under Gravity 

**Scenario:** You are analyzing a hemispherical dome structure where the material density naturally packs tighter closer to the foundational center point.

- **The Problem:** A solid hemisphere of radius $a$ lies resting above the xy-plane ($z \ge 0$), centered at the origin. The density varies continuously based on the distance from the core:
    
    $$\rho(x, y, z) = c\sqrt{x^2 + y^2 + z^2}$$
    
    where $c$ is a physical constant. Find the coordinates of its center of mass $(\overline{x}, \overline{y}, \overline{z})$.
    
- **What this tests:** Spherical coordinate transformations, managing the complex spherical Jacobian ($dV = \rho^2 \sin\phi \, d\rho \, d\phi \, d\theta$), and tracking variable expressions.
    
- **Note:** Keep your density variable ($\rho$) visually distinct from the spherical coordinate radius ($\rho$) so your formulas don't get tangled up!
