Rotational Inertia of a Parabolic Flywheel 

**Scenario:** An aerospace engineer is optimizing a high-speed flywheel rotor shaped like a paraboloid to resist structural twisting under immense angular acceleration.

- **The Problem:** A solid spinning rotor is bounded below by the paraboloid $z = x^2 + y^2$ and bounded above by the flat plane $z = 4$. Due to centrifugal casting, the material's density increases dramatically toward the outer rim:
    
    $$\rho(x, y, z) = x^2 + y^2$$
    
    Set up and evaluate the triple integral required to find its moment of inertia $I_z$ about its rotational axis.
    
- **What this tests:** Integrating a complex second-moment integrand combined with a non-uniform density function over parabolic 3D boundaries. This requires selecting the absolute best coordinate system, correctly tracking the boundary intersections, and executing multiple steps of integration flawlessly.
