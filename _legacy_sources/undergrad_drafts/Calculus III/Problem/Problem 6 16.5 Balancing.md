Cylindrical Coordinates for a Variable Turbine Axle 

**Scenario:** A heavy industrial cylinder increases in density along its length due to composite material layering.

- **The Problem:** A solid mechanical component is shaped like a cylinder of radius $R$ and height $h$, centered perfectly along the z-axis with its base at $z = 0$. The density increases as you move up the cylinder:
    
    $$\rho(x, y, z) = 1 + z$$
    
    Use cylindrical coordinates to compute its total mass $M$ and its moment of inertia $I_z$ around its central axis of rotation.
    
- **What this tests:** Utilizing cylindrical coordinates ($dV = r \, dz \, dr \, d\theta$) and applying the 3D formula for rotational inertia about the z-axis ($I_z = \iiint_E (x^2+y^2)\rho \, dV$).
