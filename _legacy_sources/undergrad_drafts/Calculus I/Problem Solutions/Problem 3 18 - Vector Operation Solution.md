### **Solution to Problem 3**

Here is the step-by-step solution to find and verify the unit vector.

#### **Formulas**

1. **Magnitude:** To find the magnitude of a vector $\vec{v} = \langle x, y \rangle$, use the formula:
    
    $$|\vec{v}| = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}$$
    
2. **Unit Vector:** A unit vector $\hat{u}_v$ points in the same direction as $\vec{v}$ but has a magnitude of exactly $1$. It is found by dividing the vector by its magnitude:
    
    $$\hat{u}_v = \frac{\vec{v}}{|\vec{v}|} = \frac{x}{|\vec{v}|}\hat{i} + \frac{y}{|\vec{v}|}\hat{j}$$
    

#### **Step 1: Find the Magnitude of $\vec{v}$**

Given the vector $\vec{v} = \langle 6, 8 \rangle$:

$$|\vec{v}| = \sqrt{6^2 + 8^2}$$

$$|\vec{v}| = \sqrt{36 + 64}$$

$$|\vec{v}| = \sqrt{100} = 10$$

#### **Step 2: Divide the Vector by its Magnitude**

Divide each component of $\vec{v} = \langle 6, 8 \rangle$ by the magnitude $10$:

$$\hat{u}_v = \left\langle \frac{6}{10}, \frac{8}{10} \right\rangle$$

Simplify the fractions to lowest terms:

$$\hat{u}_v = \left\langle \frac{3}{5}, \frac{4}{5} \right\rangle = \langle 0.6, 0.8 \rangle$$

#### **Step 3: Verify the Magnitude Equals $1$**

To check your work, calculate the magnitude of the new unit vector $\hat{u}_v$ to ensure it equals $1$:

$$|\hat{u}_v| = \sqrt{\left(\frac{3}{5}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{4}{5}\right)^2}$$

$$|\hat{u}_v| = \sqrt{\frac{9}{25} + \frac{16}{25}}$$

$$|\hat{u}_v| = \sqrt{\frac{25}{25}} = \sqrt{1} = 1$$

#### **Final Answer**

The unit vector in the same direction is **$\langle 0.6, 0.8 \rangle$** (or **$\frac{3}{5}\hat{i} + \frac{4}{5}\hat{j}$**).
