### Solution to Problem 6: Scalar Multiplication

**Problem Statement:** Let the vector be $\vec{u} = 3\hat{i} - 2\hat{j}$. Find the component form of the scaled vector $3\vec{u}$, and describe what scalar multiplication does to the magnitude of a vector.

#### Step 1: Understand the Scalar Multiplication Rule

To multiply a vector by a scalar (a regular number), you distribute that number to every component of the vector. If $c$ is a scalar and $\vec{u} = u_x\hat{i} + u_y\hat{j}$, then:

$$c\vec{u} = (c \cdot u_x)\hat{i} + (c \cdot u_y)\hat{j}$$

#### Step 2: Substitute and Calculate

Multiply each component of $\vec{u} = 3\hat{i} - 2\hat{j}$ by the scalar $3$:

$$3\vec{u} = (3 \cdot 3)\hat{i} + (3 \cdot (-2))\hat{j}$$

$$3\vec{u} = 9\hat{i} - 6\hat{j}$$

#### Step 3: Describe the Effect on Magnitude

Scalar multiplication scales the magnitude (length) of a vector by the absolute value of that scalar factor.

We can prove this by checking the new magnitude vs. the original magnitude:

- **Original Magnitude:** $|\vec{u}| = \sqrt{3^2 + (-2)^2} = \sqrt{9 + 4} = \sqrt{13}$
    
- **New Magnitude:** $|3\vec{u}| = \sqrt{9^2 + (-6)^2} = \sqrt{81 + 36} = \sqrt{117}$
    

Since $\sqrt{117} = \sqrt{9 \times 13} = 3\sqrt{13}$, the new magnitude is exactly **3 times larger** than the original magnitude.

_(Note: Because the scalar $3$ is a positive number, the direction of the vector stays exactly the same; it just grows longer)._

#### Final Answer

The component form of the scaled vector is:

$$3\vec{u} = 9\hat{i} - 6\hat{j}$$

**Effect on Magnitude:** Multiplying a vector by a scalar of $3$ increases its magnitude by a factor of exactly $3$.
