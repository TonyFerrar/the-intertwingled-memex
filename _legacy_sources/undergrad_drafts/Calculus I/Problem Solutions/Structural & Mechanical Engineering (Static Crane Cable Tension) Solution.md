**Context:** Mechanical engineers must ensure that structural supports can hold heavy machinery. A heavy generator is suspended in place at a construction site by two overhead support cables. Cable A exerts a pulling force vector of $\vec{F}_A = \langle -8, 15 \rangle\text{ kN}$ and Cable B exerts a pulling force vector of $\vec{F}_B = \langle 8, 12 \rangle\text{ kN}$.

**Problem:** Find the resultant force vector $\vec{F}_R$ acting on the generator, and calculate its total magnitude.

**Solution:**

1. Find the resultant force vector by adding the components together:
    
    $$\vec{F}_R = \vec{F}_A + \vec{F}_B = \langle -8 + 8, 15 + 12 \rangle\text{ kN}$$
    
    $$\vec{F}_R = \langle 0, 27 \rangle\text{ kN}$$
    
2. Calculate the magnitude of the resultant force:
    
    $$|\vec{F}_R| = \sqrt{0^2 + 27^2} = 27\text{ kN}$$
    

**Meaning of the Answer & Real-Life Application:**

- **$\vec{F}_R = \langle 0, 27 \rangle\text{ kN}$** shows that the horizontal forces cancel each other out completely ($0\text{ kN}$ horizontal), meaning the generator will not swing or sway side-to-side.
    
- **$|\vec{F}_R| = 27\text{ kN}$** means the combined system is exerting a net upward force of exactly $27\text{ kN}$. In engineering design, because the system is stationary (in static equilibrium), this tells the engineer that the generator weighs exactly $27\text{ kN}$ (roughly $6,000\text{ lbs}$). The engineer uses this value to select high-strength cables that are safety-rated well above $27\text{ kN}$ to prevent catastrophic snapped-cable failures.
