### Solution to Problem 8: Vector Trigonometry and Direction Angle

**Problem Statement:** A vector $\vec{V}$ has an $x$-component of $V_x = 3$ and a $y$-component of $V_y = -2$. Write out the trigonometric formula involving the inverse tangent ($\tan^{-1}$) used to find the direction angle $\theta$, and set up the calculation to find the angle relative to the positive x-axis.

#### Step 1: Understand the Trigonometric Relationships

When a vector is plotted on a Cartesian plane, its components form a right triangle:

- The $x$-component ($V_x$) acts as the **adjacent side** to the reference angle.
    
- The $y$-component ($V_y$) acts as the **opposite side** to the reference angle.
    

From basic trigonometry, the tangent of an angle is the ratio of the opposite side to the adjacent side:

$$\tan\theta = \frac{\text{Opposite}}{\text{Adjacent}} = \frac{V_y}{V_x}$$

To isolate the angle $\theta$, we apply the inverse tangent ($\tan^{-1}$ or $\text{arctan}$) to both sides:

$$\theta = \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{V_y}{V_x}\right)$$

#### Step 2: Substitute the Given Components

From the problem statement:

- $V_x = 3$
    
- $V_y = -2$
    

Substitute these values into our formula:

$$\theta = \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{-2}{3}\right)$$

#### Step 3: Calculate the Reference Angle

Using a calculator set to **Degrees**:

$$\theta \approx \tan^{-1}(-0.6667) \approx -33.69^\circ$$

#### Step 4: Adjust for the Correct Quadrant

A positive $x$-component ($+3$) and a negative $y$-component ($-2$) place this vector firmly in **Quadrant IV**.

- The calculator output of $-33.69^\circ$ represents a clockwise rotation below the positive $x$-axis.
    
- To express this as a standard counterclockwise angle between $0^\circ$ and $360^\circ$, add $360^\circ$ to the negative angle:
    
    $$\theta_{\text{standard}} = 360^\circ - 33.69^\circ = 326.31^\circ$$
    

#### Final Answer

The fundamental formula from your notes is:

$$\theta = \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{V_y}{V_x}\right)$$

The directional angle is **$-33.69^\circ$** (or **$326.31^\circ$** when measured counterclockwise from the positive $x$-axis).
