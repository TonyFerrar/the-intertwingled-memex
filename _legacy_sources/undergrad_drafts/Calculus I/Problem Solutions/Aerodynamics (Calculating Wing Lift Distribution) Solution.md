An aerospace engineer is calculating the total aerodynamic lift generated along a experimental airplane wing that spans from $x = 0$ meters (fuselage) to $x = 8$ meters (wingtip). Wind tunnel data models the local lifting force per unit length as $L(x) = 200 - 2x^2$ (in Newtons/meter). Partition the wing into $n = 4$ equal segments and use a **Right Riemann Sum** to approximate the total lift.

#### Solution:

1. **Partition the domain:**
    
    $$\Delta x = \frac{8 - 0}{4} = 2 \text{ meters per segment}$$
    
2. **Identify right endpoints:**
    
    The intervals are $[0,2], [2,4], [4,6], [6,8]$. The right endpoints are:
    
    $$x_1 = 2, \quad x_2 = 4, \quad x_3 = 6, \quad x_4 = 8$$
    
3. **Calculate heights (Local lift at each point):**
    
    - $L(2) = 200 - 2(2)^2 = 200 - 8 = 192\text{ N/m}$
        
    - $L(4) = 200 - 2(4)^2 = 200 - 32 = 168\text{ N/m}$
        
    - $L(6) = 200 - 2(6)^2 = 200 - 72 = 128\text{ N/m}$
        
    - $L(8) = 200 - 2(8)^2 = 200 - 128 = 72\text{ N/m}$
        
4. **Compute the Riemann sum:**
    
    $$\text{Total Lift} \approx 2 \cdot (192 + 168 + 128 + 72) = 2 \cdot (560) = 1120 \text{ Newtons}$$
    

#### Meaning & Real-Life Application:

- **Meaning of the answer:** The value of $1120\text{ N}$ represents an approximation of the net upward mechanical force generated by this half of the airplane's wing.
    
- **How it applies:** Aerospace engineers use these values to ensure the wing generates enough total lift to counteract the aircraft's weight. Furthermore, by partitioning the wing into individual elements, structural engineers can calculate the mechanical "bending moment" at the root ($x=0$) to ensure the aluminum or carbon fiber wing spar is strong enough not to snap under structural loading.
