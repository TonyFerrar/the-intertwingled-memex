An commercial airliner is traveling forward with a thrust velocity vector relative to the air represented by $\vec{v}_{\text{plane}} = 240\hat{i} + 0\hat{j}$ knots (flying due east). Suddenly, the plane encounters a severe high-altitude jet stream crosswind blowing with a velocity vector of $\vec{v}_{\text{wind}} = -30\hat{i} + 45\hat{j}$ knots.

- **Task:** Find the actual resultant ground velocity vector of the airplane ($\vec{v}_{\text{ground}} = \vec{v}_{\text{plane}} + \vec{v}_{\text{wind}}$).
    

**Solution:**

1. Combine the thrust and wind vectors:
    
    $$\vec{v}_{\text{ground}} = (240 + (-30))\hat{i} + (0 + 45)\hat{j}$$
    
2. Simplify the components:
    
    $$\vec{v}_{\text{ground}} = 210\hat{i} + 45\hat{j}$$
    

- **Meaning of the Answer & Real-Life Application:** The resulting ground velocity vector of $210\hat{i} + 45\hat{j}$ means that relative to the actual ground below, the plane's forward speed east has been slowed down to $210$ knots (due to a headwind component), and it is simultaneously being drifted north at a speed of $45$ knots. In aerospace engineering and flight navigation, calculating this vector addition is vital. The flight computer uses this resultant vector to realize the plane is drifting off-course and automatically adjusts the aircraft's heading (angling the nose slightly into the wind) to ensure it reaches its exact intended destination.
