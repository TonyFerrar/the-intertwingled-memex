### (a) Find the following: 
* **i.** $\lim_{x\to-4^{-}}f(x) = \infty$ *(As you approach $x = -4$ from the left, the curve shoots upwards to positive infinity.)
* **ii.** $\lim_{x\to-4^{+}}f(x) = 6$ *(As you approach $x = -4$ from the right, the line leads directly to the open hole located at a $y$-value of $6$.)
* **iii.** $\lim_{x\to\infty}f(x) = -2$ (As $x$ heads out to the far right, the curve flattens out horizontally along the grid line $y = -2$.)
* **iv.** $\lim_{x\to3^{+}}f(x) = -\infty$ *(As you approach $x = 3$ from the right, the curve shoots downwards to negative infinity.)
* **v.** $\lim_{x\to3^{-}}f(x) = \infty$ *(As you approach $x = 3$ from the left, the curve shoots upwards to positive infinity.)
* **vi.** $\lim_{x\to-\infty}f(x) = 1$ *(As $x$ heads out to the far left, the curve flattens out horizontally along the grid line $y = 1$.)
---
### (b) State any horizontal asymptotes for $f(x)$. 
The function approaches flat horizontal lines as $x$ goes to positive and negative infinity. 
The equations for these lines are: 
$$y = 1$$ 
$$y = -2$$

---

### (c) State any vertical asymptotes for $f(x)$. 
The function shoots off to infinity or negative infinity around certain vertical lines. 
Looking closely at the behavior around the breaks: * 
**At $x = -4$:** The left side goes to $\infty$, making **$x = -4$** a vertical asymptote (even though the right side ends at a hole). * 
**At $x = 3$:** The left side goes to $\infty$ and the right side goes to $-\infty$, making **$x = 3$** a vertical asymptote. 
Equations for the vertical asymptotes: * $$x = -4$$ * $$x = 3$$
