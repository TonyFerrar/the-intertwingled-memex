**Context:** Civil engineers design structural trusses to distribute weight safely across bridges. During a load test, a steel support diagonal experiences a compressive force vector $\vec{F}$ with a total magnitude of $45\text{ kN}$ acting at an angle of $35^\circ$ below the horizontal, pointing toward a concrete ground junction.

**Problem:** Decompose this structural force vector into its horizontal and vertical component forces.

**Solution:**

Because the force is pushing downward and to the right into the junction, the angle in standard position is $-35^\circ$ (or $325^\circ$).

- **Horizontal Force ($F_x$):**
    
    $$F_x = 45\cos(-35^\circ) \approx 36.86\text{ kN}$$
    
- **Vertical Force ($F_y$):**
    
    $$F_y = 45\sin(-35^\circ) \approx -25.81\text{ kN}$$
    

In component form: $\vec{F} = \langle 36.86, -25.81 \rangle\text{ kN}$.

**Meaning of the Answer & Real-Life Application:**

- **$F_x = 36.86\text{ kN}$** means the bridge truss is pushing sideways against the concrete foundation with $36.86\text{ kN}$ of force. The civil engineer uses this value to calculate how much friction or anchoring shear-strength the foundation needs so the bridge doesn't slide outward.
    
- **$F_y = -25.81\text{ kN}$** represents the downward load transferred directly into the ground. The engineer uses this value to determine the thickness of the concrete pad so it doesn't crack or sink into the soil under the weight.
