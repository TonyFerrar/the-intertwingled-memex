A civil engineer is inspecting a support cable on a suspension bridge truss. The force vector being exerted by the cable on an anchor pin is recorded by sensors as $\vec{F} = 15\hat{i} - 36\hat{j}$ kilonewtons (kN). The structural safety manual states that this specific anchor pin will sheer and fail if it experiences a total absolute force exceeding $40$ kN.

- **Task:** Calculate the total absolute magnitude of the force vector, and determine if the anchor pin is safe.
    

**Solution:**

1. Identify the components of the force vector: $F_x = 15$ and $F_y = -36$.
    
2. Calculate the absolute magnitude of the force:
    
    $$|\vec{F}| = \sqrt{(15)^2 + (-36)^2}$$
    
    $$|\vec{F}| = \sqrt{225 + 1296} = \sqrt{1521}$$
    
    $$|\vec{F}| = 39\text{ kN}$$
    
3. Compare to safety limits: $39\text{ kN} < 40\text{ kN}$.
    

- **Meaning of the Answer & Real-Life Application:** The calculated magnitude of $39\text{ kN}$ is the total physical pull felt by the steel anchor pin from all direction combined. In structural engineering, computing the scalar magnitude from vector components is vital because physical materials fail based on the total combined stress they undergo. Because $39\text{ kN}$ is just below the structural threshold of $40$ kN, the engineer determines the pin is currently operating safely, though it is dangerously close to its mechanical limit and should be scheduled for reinforcement.
