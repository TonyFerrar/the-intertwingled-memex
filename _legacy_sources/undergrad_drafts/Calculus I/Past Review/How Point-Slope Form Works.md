# Understanding Point-Slope Form: A Step-by-Step Guide

The **point-slope form** is one of the most powerful and intuitive ways to write the equation of a straight line. It is especially useful when you know a specific point on the line and how steep the line is (its slope).

---

## Core Concepts

### What is Point-Slope Form?
The point-slope form of a linear equation is written as:
$$y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)$$



#### Breaking Down the Components:
* **$m$**: The **slope** of the line, which measures its steepness ("rise over run").
* **$(x_1, y_1)$**: A **specific, known point** that lies anywhere on the line.
* **$x$ and $y$**: The **variables** that remain in the final equation, representing *any* coordinate pair $(x, y)$ on that line.

### Why Does It Work?
The formula comes directly from the definition of slope ($m$). The slope formula between any random point $(x, y)$ and our fixed point $(x_1, y_1)$ is:
$$m = \frac{y - y_1}{x - x_1}$$

If you multiply both sides by the denominator $(x - x_1)$, you isolate the $y$-terms and get the point-slope form:
$$m(x - x_1) = y - y_1 \implies y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)$$

---

## How to Use It (Step-by-Step)

When asked to find the equation of a line using this form, you generally follow three steps:
1. **Identify or calculate the slope ($m$).**
2. **Identify a point on the line ($(x_1, y_1)$).**
3. **Plug them into the formula** and simplify if your instructor wants it in a different format (like slope-intercept form, $y = mx + b$).

---

## Worked Examples

### Example 1: Given a Point and a Slope
Find the equation of the line that passes through the point $(1, 7)$ with a slope of $\frac{2}{3}$. [cite_start]Present your answer in slope-intercept form[cite: 34, 35, 36].

#### Step-by-Step Solution:

**1. Extract the given information:**
* [cite_start]Known point: $(x_1, y_1) = (1, 7)$ [cite: 34]
* [cite_start]Slope: $m = \frac{2}{3}$ [cite: 35]

**2. Substitute the values into the point-slope formula:**
$$y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)$$
$$y - 7 = \frac{2}{3}(x - 1)$$

**3. Convert to slope-intercept form ($y = mx + b$):**
* First, distribute the slope $\frac{2}{3}$ into the parentheses:
  $$y - 7 = \frac{2}{3}x - \frac{2}{3}$$
* Next, isolate $y$ by adding $7$ to both sides:
  $$y = \frac{2}{3}x - \frac{2}{3} + 7$$
* To combine the constants, convert $7$ to a fraction with a common denominator of $3$ ($\frac{21}{3}$):
  $$y = \frac{2}{3}x - \frac{2}{3} + \frac{21}{3}$$
  $$y = \frac{2}{3}x + \frac{19}{3}$$

---

### Example 2: Handling Negative Values
Find the equation of the line with a slope of $-4$ that passes through the point $(-2, 5)$.

#### Step-by-Step Solution:

**1. Extract the given information:**
* Known point: $(x_1, y_1) = (-2, 5)$
* Slope: $m = -4$

**2. Substitute the values into the formula:**
* Be extra careful with signs when plugging in negative coordinates:
  $$y - 5 = -4(x - (-2))$$
* Simplify the double negative inside the parentheses ($x - (-2) \implies x + 2$):
  $$y - 5 = -4(x + 2)$$

**3. Convert to slope-intercept form:**
* Distribute the $-4$:
  $$y - 5 = -4x - 8$$
* Add $5$ to both sides to solve for $y$:
  $$y = -4x - 8 + 5$$
  $$y = -4x - 3$$

---

### Example 3: Given Two Points
Find the equation of the line that passes through the points $(3, 4)$ and $(5, 8)$.

#### Step-by-Step Solution:

**1. Find the slope ($m$) first:**
* Use the slope formula $m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$ with points $(3, 4)$ and $(5, 8)$:
  $$m = \frac{8 - 4}{5 - 3} = \frac{4}{2} = 2$$

**2. Pick one point to act as your $(x_1, y_1)$:**
* You can pick *either* point; both will yield the exact same final equation. Let's pick $(3, 4)$.
* Now we have $m = 2$ and $(x_1, y_1) = (3, 4)$.

**3. Substitute into the point-slope formula:**
$$y - 4 = 2(x - 3)$$

**4. Convert to slope-intercept form:**
* Distribute the $2$:
  $$y - 4 = 2x - 6$$
* Add $4$ to both sides:
  $$y = 2x - 2$$

---

## When to Use Which Linear Form

| Equation Form | Formula | Best Used When... |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Point-Slope** | $y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)$ | You know a point and a slope, or two random points. Excellent for starting a problem. |
| **Slope-Intercept** | $y = mx + b$ | You know the specific $y$-intercept $(0, b)$ and slope, or you want to graph the line easily. |
| **Standard Form** | $Ax + By = C$ | You want to find intercepts quickly ($x=0$ or $y=0$), or write equations for modeling word problems. |
