# Session 04 + Infinite Limits

## Engineering Application 
This module bridges abstract calculus limits with physical reality by matching mathematical behaviors to core engineering principles. Infinite limits and their corresponding vertical asymptotes act as structural constraints, mapping phenomena like **mechanical resonance** in civil engineering where forces grow without bound near a critical driving frequency, risking catastrophic structural failure. Conversely, limits at infinity and their corresponding horizontal asymptotes establish long-term operational thresholds and physical bottlenecks. These are used by aerospace engineers to calculate the **terminal velocity** of a falling payload under balanced drag forces, by electrical engineers to determine the steady-state maximum voltage across a **charging capacitor**, and by chemical engineers utilizing the Michaelis-Menten model to find the theoretical **maximum speed ($V_{\max}$)** of a catalyzed biochemical reaction regardless of infinite raw material input.
