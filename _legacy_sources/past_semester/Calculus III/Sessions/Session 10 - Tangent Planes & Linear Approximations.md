# Session 10 - Tangent Planes & Linear Approximations

## Session overview 

Engineers often work with complex systems that behave in highly nonlinear ways, but when operating near a known state these systems can be simplified dramatically. Tangent planes and linear approximations provide exactly this tool: a flat, local model that captures the immediate behavior of a surface. Mechanical engineers use them to approximate how small adjustments in geometry affect stress or vibration. Civil engineers use them to estimate how loads shift on a structure without running a full simulation. Electrical engineers use them when tuning circuits around a bias point, where nonlinear elements like diodes or transistors behave almost linearly. Environmental engineers use them to predict pollutant concentrations or flow rates under slight changes in conditions. By replacing a complicated surface with a tangent plane, engineers can make fast predictions, design quick adjustments, and build intuition for how systems respond—without sacrificing accuracy in the neighborhood of interest.
