# Session 13 - Optimization with Constraints

## Session Overview
Every engineering design involves competing goals: stronger structures cost more, faster circuits generate more heat, and safer systems are often heavier or less efficient. Optimization with constraints gives engineers a mathematical way to navigate those trade-offs. By expressing performance or cost as an objective function and limits as constraint equations, we can identify the best possible design within real-world restrictions. The method of **Lagrange multipliers** provides a powerful tool for doing this—quantifying exactly how much improvement in one goal “costs” in another. Whether minimizing material in a bridge, balancing loudness and clarity in a concert venue, or maximizing fluid flow under pressure limits, these techniques turn intuition about compromise into precise, testable design decisions.
