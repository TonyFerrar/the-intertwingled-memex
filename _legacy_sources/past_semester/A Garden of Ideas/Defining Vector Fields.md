### The General Vector Field
$$\mathbf{F}(x,y,z)=\langle P(x,y,z),\,Q(x,y,z),\,R(x,y,z)\rangle$$
$$ = P(x,y,z) \hat{i} + Q(x,y,z) \hat{j} + R(x,y,z) \hat{k}$$

A **vector field** is a function that assigns a vector to every point in space.  
Each component—$P$, $Q$, and $R$—describes how the field behaves along the $x$, $y$, and $z$ directions.

- **2-D fields** omit the third component: $\mathbf{F}(x,y)=\langle P(x,y),\,Q(x,y)\rangle$.  
- The vector $\mathbf{F}(x_0,y_0,z_0)$ shows the local direction and strength of the field at a specific point.  

*Engineering view:* The arrows you draw represent wind, current, or force vectors distributed throughout a region.

### Magnitude of a Vector Field
$$|\mathbf{F}|=\sqrt{P^2+Q^2+R^2}$$

The **magnitude** (or **strength**) of the field at each point measures how strong the effect is there.  

- In a **velocity field**, $|\mathbf{F}|$ tells how fast the fluid moves.  
- In a **force field**, $|\mathbf{F}|$ gives the force intensity.  

When sketching, longer arrows correspond to larger magnitudes.
