
# Derivatives of Parametric Equations

We care about taking derivatives of parametric equations because it allows us to easily calculate the **slope of a tangent line** to a curve at a specific point, even if we only know the parameter's value. Conceptually, this is achieved by applying standard differentiation rules—specifically the chain rule—to parametric curves. Instead of needing to eliminate the parameter to find a standard Cartesian function first, you can find the rate of change by taking the derivative of both the $x$ function and the $y$ function individually with respect to the parameter $t$.

The core mathematical rule for this is the **Parametric Curves Derivative Theorem**. It states that if you have differentiable functions $x=f(t)$ and $y=g(t)$, the slope of the line tangent to the curve is calculated with the formula
$$\frac{dx}{dy}​ = \frac{dx}{dt} \cdot \frac{dy}{dt}$$
​provided that the denominator $\frac{dt}{dx​}$ is not equal to zero.
