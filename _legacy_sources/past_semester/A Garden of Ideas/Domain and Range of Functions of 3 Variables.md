
# Domain and Range of Functions of 3 Variables


When moving from 2 independent variables ($z=z(x,y)$) to 3 independent variables ($w=w(x,y,z)$), the basic rules for domain and range still apply.  

What changes is **how we represent and visualize** them.

**1. Domain = Volumes**  
- In 2 variables, domains are regions in the plane.  
- In 3 variables, domains are **solid regions** in space (balls, half-spaces, etc.).  

**2. Graphing is No Longer a Surface**  
- For 2 variables, graphs are surfaces in 3D.  
- For 3 variables, we can’t directly “graph” in 4D.  
- Instead, we use **level surfaces**: sets of points where $f(x,y,z)=c$.  

**3. Level Surfaces vs. Level Curves**  
- Level curves (2D) → contour lines on a surface.  
- Level surfaces (3D) → familiar solids (planes, spheres, cylinders).  
- Students should learn to “read” these shapes as slices through 3D space.  

**4. Cross-Sections**  
- To understand a 3-variable function, take slices by fixing one variable.  
- Example: Fix $z=0$, study $f(x,y,0)$ like a 2-variable function.  

**5. Visualization Strategy**  
- Think in terms of **families of surfaces** rather than a single graph.  
- Use symmetry, intercepts, and cross-sections to picture shapes.  

**6. Applications Get Richer**  
- Many physical laws (heat, pressure, electric potential) naturally live in 3 variables.  
- Recognize that moving to 3D is not “just harder math,” but a closer model of the real world.  

> [!warning] Note for the Scope of Multivariable Functions in this class
> For the sake of convenience, the most amount of variables for a given function you'll be tasked with working with in this class will be three variables. However, all domain rules also expand to functions with more than three variables.
