
# Things Successful Math Students Do
In this course, success isn’t about being a “math genius.” It’s about building habits that let you grow, adapt, and thrive. Over the years, I’ve noticed that the strongest students share a common set of practices. I call them the **Six Pillars of Success**:

## Planning
Life is complicated. Success doesn’t happen by accident—it happens when you follow a plan that works for _you_. Planning is about making intentional choices with your time so you can move all areas of your life forward.

## Preparing
Learning requires intention. The students who get the most out of class don’t walk in cold—they arrive with the day’s materials in hand, ready to engage. Preparing before class saves you time later and lets you unlock the _maximum value_ from each session.

## Participating
Learning is not a spectator sport. The sooner you stop seeing yourself as a passive recipient of information, the sooner you’ll feel ahead of the game. Participation means connecting with your classmates, asking questions, and diving into the work together. The more you engage, the easier and more rewarding the learning becomes.

## Processing
New ideas pass in and out of our brains quickly. The best students take a few minutes _after class_ to review notes, highlight key takeaways, and reflect on what they just learned. These small habits cement understanding and prevent frustration later.

## Practicing
Watching someone solve a problem is not the same as solving it yourself. Just like learning to ride a bike or play an instrument, math requires _doing_. Practice builds confidence, reduces test anxiety, and prepares you for the kinds of messy, real-world problems engineers face.

## Playing
You’re a whole human being. No amount of drive can sustain you through a semester-long grind without rest. Taking breaks, spending time on hobbies, or hanging out with friends is not wasted time - it’s essential recovery. Just like muscles grow during rest, your brain consolidates learning when you unplug.

## Reflection Opportunities
This semester, you’ll have chances to explore your own approach to learning and reflect on what’s working for you. My goal is to help you not only succeed in this class but also develop skills that will serve you for life.
