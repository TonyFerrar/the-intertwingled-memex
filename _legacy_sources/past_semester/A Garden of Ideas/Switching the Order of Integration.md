# Switching the Order of Integration

Often, the order in which a double integral is performed can dramatically change the process, making it simpler or more complicated depending on the order chosen.
- In [[Double Integrals over a Rectangular Region]], you learned how to switch the order over rectangular regions. This was mainly done to simplify the integration process based on the nuances of the **integrand**.
- In [[Double Integrals over a General Region]], you encountered limits of integration that are themselves functions. This page explores the ways that switching the order of integration can simplify things, based on the **limits of integration**.

In general regions, the process of switching the order of integration becomes more complicated - merely switching which integral you perform first is no longer sufficient.


> [!warning] Important Notes for switching bounds
> When switching the order of integration over a general region, the region **switches** from a type 1 to a type 2 region (or vice versa). This means that when switching the order of integration, you must convert the bounds from $y(x)$ to $x(y)$ (i.e. **inverting the functions** within your bounds)
> 
> As was mentioned in [[Double Integrals over a General Region]], you **always** integrate the bounds defined by a function ($y(x)$ or $x(y)$) first, **then** move to constants.
