
# Points-Based Grading is Confusing
Imagine that you're a professor, sitting down to design a course syllabus. What percentage of the course grade should homework count for? Exams? If you ask 100 professors, you'll probably get 100 slightly different answers. **There is no centralized standard** for grade distributions at most major colleges and universities.

To make matters worse, imagine you're writing the final exam for your course. How many questions should appear? Should they all be worth the same number of points, or are there some topics that should be worth more because they are more important? Again, **there is no centralized standard**, which means the final exam from one section of a course could be entirely different from the exam in another.

Now imagine you're sitting down to grade an exam. A student made a minor algebra error. How many points should you take off? Ask 100 professors, you're again likely to get 100 different answers.

If you put this all together, you start to see the problem: the same student who learns the same skills could earn wildly different grades depending on who their professor is.

From the student perspective: rather than focusing purely on learning the skills in a course, you're stuck doing complicated syllabus math. Does "I need to score at least xx% on the final to get a B" sound familiar? What control do you have over your score anyhow? After all, it will be the professor who grades your exam who determines that. The only thing you control is the skill you build ahead of the exam.

That's why [[Contract Grading]] is an exciting alternative - it adds clarity to your score, ties it tightly to the effort you invest and the skill you build, and ultimately places you in the driver's seat of your own learning journey.
