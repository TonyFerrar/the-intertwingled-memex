
# Evaluating and Visualizing Parametric Equations

Parametric equations are highly valuable because they simplify the representation of complex curves and trajectories that are difficult or impossible to express as single, real-valued functions. For example, tracking a fixed point on a freely rolling wheel (known as a cycloid) results in an incredibly complex standard equation, but becomes much easier to calculate when using parametric representation. 

Conceptually, rather than directly relating $x$ and $y$, parametric curves introduce a third variable called a **parameter** (often $t$ for time or $\theta$ for an angle), such that both the $x$ and $y$ coordinates depend entirely on this new variable. By plugging in a sample of values for the parameter, you can evaluate the coordinates to easily sketch the curve's shape and determine its **orientation**, or the direction the curve follows as the parameter increases.

The general definition of a parametric curve is defined by the key formulas:

> [!info] Parametric Equations - Essential Formulas
> 
$x=f(t)$ and $y=g(t)$, over a given interval $a≤t≤b$.

Specific applications include the general parametric equation of a straight line, defined as $x=x_0​+at$ and $y=y_0​+bt$, and the parametric representation of a unit circle, which relies on trigonometric functions where $x=\cos(\theta)$ and $y=\sin(\theta)$.
