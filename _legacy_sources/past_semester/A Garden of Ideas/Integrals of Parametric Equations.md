# Integrals of Parametric Equations

Just as derivatives can be adapted for parametric curves, definite integrals can be used to calculate the **area under a parametric curve** without needing to rewrite the curve as a standard $y=f(x)$ function. Conceptually, this relies on a generalization of the $u$-substitution method to rewrite the standard area integral $\int y \, dx$ entirely in terms of the parameter $t$.

This is formally defined by the **Area Under a Parametric Curve's Theorem**. For a curve parametrized by $x=f(t)$ and $y=g(t)$ over the interval $a≤t≤b$, the area under the curve is given by the key formula
$$A=\int_a^b ​g(t) \, f'(t) \,dt$$

In this formula, $g(t)$ substitutes for $y$, and $f'(t)dt$ substitutes for $dx$.
