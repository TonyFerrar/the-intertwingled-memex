# What Do Grades Incentivize?

A primary challenge in education is the struggle between [[extrinsic motivation]] and [[intrinsic motivation]]. Many of the structures we employ in the name of establishing standards have the negative effect of driving students to seek external recognition over their own sense of curiosity and achievement.

## [Computer Lib/Dream Machines](https://archive.org/details/computer-lib-dream-machines)
This is not a new issue. A decade before I was born, Ted Nelson published a book that is now considered to be the first book ever written about the personal computer. In a time where computers were the size of offices, [Computer Lib/Dream Machines](https://archive.org/details/computer-lib-dream-machines) imagined what might be possible if everyone had access. What would change in our thinking? How would we operate? Today, we have the benefit of hindsight. When it was written, [Computer Lib/Dream Machines](https://archive.org/details/computer-lib-dream-machines) shaped the direction we'd all end up going. Sadly, we've also found many ways to craft a future far from the ideals he imagined.

### What Does Nelson Say About School?
This passage presents a rather scathing review of the American Education system. If you didn't know any better, you'd think this could have been written today. In 50 years, we've made little progress toward what matters while chasing the easier to measure results, such as GPAs and standardized testing scores.

>... These writers describe particularly ghastly examples of our schooling conditions. But such horror stories aside, we are coming to recognize that schools as we know them appear designed at every level to sabotage the supposed goals of education. A child arrives at school bright and early in his life. By drabness we deprive him of interests.

>By fixed curriculum and sequence we rob him of his orientation, initiative and motivation, and by testing and scoring we subvert his natural intelligence. 

> Schools as we know them all run on the same principles: iron all subjects flat and then proceed, in groups, at a forced march across the flattened plain. Material is dumped on the students and their responses calibrated; their interaction and involvement with the material is not encouraged nor taken into consideration, but their dutifulness of response is carefully monitored.

>While an exact arrangement of intended motivations for the student is preset within the system, they do not usually take effect according to the ideal. ***It is not that students are unmotivated, but motivated askew.*** Rather than seek to achieve in the way they are supposed to, students turn to churlishness, surliness, or intellectual sheepishness. 

>A general human motivation is god-given at the beginning and warped or destroyed by the educational process as we know it; ***thus we internalize at last that most fundamental of grownup goals: just to get through another day.***

Ted Nelson, [Computer Lib/Dream Machines](https://archive.org/details/computer-lib-dream-machines), 1974.

The point that I'd like to emphasize is that grades motivate students askew. Grades ***do*** motivate us, through a blend of fear and achievement. Unfortunately, grades don't motivate us to learn - they motivate us to perform according to an externally-imposed standard.

## [The Case Against Grades](https://www.alfiekohn.org/article/case-grades/)
In his article, [The Case Against Grades](https://www.alfiekohn.org/article/case-grades/), Alfie Kohn explores the effect of grades from a more modern perspective. He confirms what Nelson predicted. Thankfully, he presents a vision for a future in which students' motivations are aligned with the values educators aspire to. By changing the way we motivate students and hold them accountable for learning, we have an opportunity to shift away from grade-grubbing and toward learning for its own sake.

While [[Contract Grading]] stops short of the narrative-feedback approach that Kohn suggests, it is a step in the right direction. I hope that in the future, maybe with the power of AI, we'll be able to assess learning and qualifications with far more nuance than the sorry attempt we make now (reducing all of a student's learning to a single number, the GPA).
