
# Session 6 - Areas and Volumes by Slicing

## Session Overview
This session explores the application of definite integrals to calculate **areas between curves** and the **volumes of complex solids**. We will establish that the area between two continuous functions is found by integrating the difference between the upper and lower curves (or right and left curves) over a specified interval. We will then extend this concept to determine volumes using the **slicing method**, which integrates the area of a typical cross-section—such as a square or semi-circle—perpendicular to an axis. Furthermore, we will examine **solids of revolution**, utilizing the **disk method** for solid objects and the **washer method** for hollow structures. Beyond these mathematical foundations, these principles are essential in engineering for calculating material volumes for non-uniform structures, designing rotational components like axles and pistons, and determining the capacity or wall volume of industrial piping systems. Areas between curves have meaning in applications ranging from thermodynamics to city planning.
