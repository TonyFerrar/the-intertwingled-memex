
# Session 9 - Applications to Physics and Biology

## Session Overview
This session explores how **integral calculus** serves as a vital tool for modeling real-world phenomena in **physics and biology**. For physics, we will learn about **work** - defined as the accumulation of force over a distance, using **definite integrals** to calculate energy requirements for moving objects, stretching springs via **Hooke’s Law**, or lifting heavy cables. Transitioning to life sciences, we'll see how integration tracks **population changes** by analyzing rates of exponential growth and decay over time. Lastly, we will explore specialized medical applications, such as using the **dye dilution method** and Riemann sums to determine **cardiac output**. By bridging theoretical math with practical examples, the document illustrates how the **accumulation process** solves complex problems involving variable rates and forces.
