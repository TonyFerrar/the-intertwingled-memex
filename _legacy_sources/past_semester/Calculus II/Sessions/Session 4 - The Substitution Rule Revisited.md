
# Session 4 - The Substitution Rule Revisited

## Session Overview
The **Substitution Rule** serves as a vital mathematical tool for simplifying "complex" integrals by reversing the **Chain Rule** of differentiation. This technique requires identifying an **"inner function" ($u$)** within an integrand and its derivative ($du$) to transform a difficult expression into a more manageable form, a process applicable to both **indefinite and definite integrals**. 

You'll explore a systematic five-step approach: selecting a $u$ variable, calculating $du$, substituting all terms (including **adjusting limits of integration** for definite integrals), evaluating the simplified integral, and, if necessary, returning to the original variable. This method handles various functional forms, including **trigonometric, exponential, and radical expressions**, sometimes requiring algebraic manipulation to solve for the original variable in terms of $u$. 

In **engineering**, this rule is indispensable because it allows for the analytical solution of models describing physical phenomena (such as **fluid dynamics, structural stress, or electrical circuits**) where the governing equations often involve composite relationships that are not integrable through basic power rules.
