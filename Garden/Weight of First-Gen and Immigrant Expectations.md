# Weight of First-Gen and Immigrant Expectations

> [!abstract] Problem Statement
> Over half of UNLV's student body consists of first-generation college students, many from immigrant families navigating higher education without a familial roadmap. Carrying the financial and emotional weight of their family's collective sacrifices, a minor academic stumble triggers deep imposter syndrome in students who feel they cannot afford to fail.

> [!tip] Measurable Goals
> Demystify the "hidden curriculum" of higher education so First-Gen and immigrant students navigate academic pathways with complete clarity, zero imposter syndrome, and total agency over their academic standing and time commitments.

---

### Course Solutions & Policies

* **Active In-Class Learning:** High-impact, peer-driven problem solving during 75-minute sessions drastically reduces the cognitive and time load required for outside homework.
* **Demystifying Syllabus & "University Meta":** Course syllabi explicitly explain university structures, academic norms, and degree progression alongside standard course policies.
* **$2\text{--}3$ Hours Per Credit Baseline Design:** Course requirements are explicitly calibrated around the official university guideline ($6\text{--}9$ hours outside work/week for a 3-credit course), protecting students from workload inflation.
* **Transparent Canvas & Monte Carlo Grade Weights:** Grade categories are weighted (60% Exams, 20% HW, 15% Memos/Redlines, 5% Engagement) and validated via Monte Carlo simulations to ensure predictable, transparent grade trajectories.
* **Omnipresent Office Hours:** Flexible office hours offered across multiple days and hybrid/online formats remove structural barriers to seeking assistance.
