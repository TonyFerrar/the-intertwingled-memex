# Inclusive Admissions Mirage and Legacy Faculty Wall

> [!abstract] Problem Statement
> Admissions offices have opened doors to a broader student body, but upper-division teaching practices remain unyielding. Legacy faculty who experienced a traditional college runway often view modern student survival strategies as a lack of commitment. Because these legacy systems will not adapt, lower-division courses must serve as a tactical shield—training resilient students to master the professional habits, self-advocacy, and stamina required to thrive under traditional faculty down the road.

> [!tip] Measurable Goals
> Equip students to survive and excel in traditionally taught upper-division courses (lecture, weekly homework, high-stakes exams, zero grace) by guiding them to discover, build, and test personalized learning and time-management strategies that work best for each individual.

---

### Course Solutions & Policies

* **Personalized Problem-Solving Flowcharts:** Homework focus shifts from "getting right answers" to building a repeatable process/flowchart for solving novel engineering problems under pressure.
* **Explicit Time-Guarding Instruction:** Classroom instruction explicitly teaches students how to budget and guard their $2\text{--}3$ hours per credit/week, including tactical protocols for when problem-solving moves slower than time allotments.
* **Professional Deliverable Standards:** Technical memos and redline audits condition students to produce clean, rigorous documentation that satisfies traditional upper-division professors.
