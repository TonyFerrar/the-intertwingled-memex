# Mirage of Hands-On Engineering

> [!abstract] Problem Statement
> Students who survive the prerequisite grind arrive expecting to prototype and build physical objects, only to hit another wall of passive lectures and scratchwork. They lack the realization that engineering is not physical tinkering; it is mathematically modeling and optimizing designs on paper *before* physical build.

> [!tip] Measurable Goals
> Reframe student perceptions away from physical mechanics ("holding wrenches" or "trial-and-error tinkering") toward recognizing that building mathematical models to predict and optimize performance on paper—and communicating those models cleanly—is the true safety-critical core of engineering.

---

### Course Solutions & Policies

* **Bi-Weekly Redline Audits (15% Category):** Students audit, redline, and correct flawed calculation sets using professional engineering QA/QC markup standards.
* **Midterm Exam Redlines:** Replaces scratchwork with structured post-mortems analyzing technical communication, notation, and calculation errors.
* **Interactive Design Decision-Making:** Group problem-solving requires students to interpret calculated mathematical results specifically to make engineering design choices, explicitly contrasting paper optimization with wasteful "trial-and-error" tinkering.
